### Mission Statement

Technology has the ability to liberate humanity, instead of ushering in a dystopian future. 
Cyber Citizens Without Borders exists to advance digital freedom, technological independence, and the fundamental right of every individual to understand, control, and shape the technology that governs their lives.

We believe that open-source software, Linux, and decentralized technologies are the foundations of a free digital society. In an increasingly controlled digital landscape—where proprietary platforms restrict access, corporations dictate the boundaries of innovation, and surveillance threatens individual autonomy—we stand for an alternative: an open, transparent, and accessible technological ecosystem that belongs to its users.

Our mission is to empower individuals to break free from technological dependence by providing a structured framework for self-directed learning, professional-grade educational resources, and practical pathways into cybersecurity, software engineering, systems administration, networking, digital privacy, and emerging technologies.

We believe knowledge should have no borders, expertise should have no gatekeepers, and technological freedom should not be reserved for those with institutional access or financial privilege.

Through open-source collaboration, independent research, hands-on experimentation, and community-driven education, we seek to cultivate a generation of technically capable, security-conscious, and digitally sovereign citizens.

We do not believe people should simply learn how to use technology. We believe they should learn how to understand it, build it, secure it, challenge it, and ultimately control it.

Our commitment is to an internet that remains open, systems that remain transparent, knowledge that remains accessible, and technology that serves humanity rather than the other way around.

No borders. No gatekeepers. No technological masters.

## Core philosophical pillars

I would build the project's identity around five foundational principles:

1. Digital Sovereignty — Individuals should own their digital identities, control their systems, protect their information, and make independent technological decisions.

2. Open-Source Absolutism — Favor transparent, auditable, modifiable, and community-maintained software over proprietary systems that restrict user autonomy.

3. Knowledge Without Borders — Professional-grade technical education should be accessible regardless of geography, socioeconomic status, institutional affiliation, or prior experience.

4. Technological Self-Determination — Teach people to build, operate, maintain, secure, and understand their own technological infrastructure rather than depend on closed ecosystems.

5. Community Over Corporations — Advance a collaborative culture where knowledge is shared, innovation is decentralized, and technological progress benefits everyone.
